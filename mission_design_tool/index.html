This tool is designed to built intuition about nadir-pointing ice-penetrating radar sounder instruments on novel platforms. Start from a preset below or enter custom system parameters to estimate the expected signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) for a radar sounder imaging the basal interface (bedrock topography or other sub-ice interface) across the Antarctic and Greenland ice sheets.

The most difficult part of building an radar sounder link budget is estimating what happens within the ice. Both attenuation within ice and reflectivity at the ice-bed interface can vary dramatically. This tool uses a statistical model of Required Surface SNR (RSSNR) to estimate the signal-to-noise ratio of a radar sounder across a wide range of possible instrument and platform configurations.

In short, RSSNR provides the expected difference between surface power return and basal power return, after correcting for geoemetric spreading. Once we have RSSNR, we can calculate the expected bed SNR using only properties of the instrument and the platform its on (aircraft, UAV, satellite, etc). That's what this tool does. 

SNRbed  =  SNRsurface  +  Gpulse-comp  +  Δspreading(d)  −  RSSNR

We treat the surface SNR as fixed (a loose approximation) and only the last two terms vary spatially, though ice thickness (derived from BedMachine) and the fitted RSSNR.

Note that these values carry a lot of uncertainty. This is intended as a general tool to build intuition about what systems perform best for what general use cases. Take care and verify local conditions before relying on it for specific predictions.

Detailed assumptions
  • Coherent Fresnel-zone summation. A 1/R² radar equation, assuming returns add coherently over the first Fresnel zone.
  • Azimuth gain is evaluated at the surface Fresnel zone, which is mildly conservative (especially at low altitudes).
  • RSSNR includes no estimate of frequency-dependence which may bias results when the center frequency is far from the frequency of airborne radar sounders represented in OPR.
  • Pulse-compression gain cancels inside RSSNR — it applies to the surface and bed returns alike — so it is added back once here, for the bed.
  • The noise floor is sky-dominated at VHF. Antenna temperature defaults to the galactic background (ITU-R P.372) — ≈3700 K at 60 MHz — or 270 K, whichever is higher.
  • Clutter is not modelled. Where the ice is thin enough that the bed echo returns before the pulse ends, the tool charges the bed for the compressed surface return's sidelobe pedestal — but surface roughness scatter arrives over the same delays and is often larger. The overlap penalty is a lower bound on the interference, not the whole of it.
  • The model has real uncertainty ( cross-validated RMSE). The 20th-percentile layer and the posterior-predictive distributions both carry it; the median layer does not.

What is RSSNR?

Required Surface SNR (RSSNR) is an engineering metric used to empirically estimate link budgets. It was originally introduced by Schroeder et al., 2021. The values used here are interpolated using a Bayesian regression. Read more about RSSNR in our docs.

Map

Don't forget about clutter

This tool does not model off-nadir clutter or any kind of englacial reflections. For high-altitude systems, clutter from the surface, firn layers, or even other parts of the basal interface may become the limiting factors for basal interface detection.

Coverage

Data citation — the radar metrics behind the RSSNR model are derived from the Open Polar Radar catalog, read with xOPR and reduced by englacial/radar-return-statistics. Please cite Open Polar Radar and the underlying campaign data providers when using these results.

The model, the covariate grid and this viewer are produced by englacial/radar-return-statistics-postprocessing. Every external dataset joined onto the grid — BedMachine, MEaSUREs and ITS_LIVE surface velocity, ERA5, and geothermal heat flow — carries its own citation requirements, listed at docs/data_sources.md.

The required-surface-SNR approach was introduced by Schroeder, D.M., Bienert, N.L., Culberg, R., MacKie, E.J., Teisberg, T.O., Chu, W., Young, D.A. (2021): Glaciological Constraints on Link Budgets for Orbital Radar Sounding of Earth's Ice Sheets, in: 2021 IEEE International Geoscience and Remote Sensing Symposium IGARSS, IEEE, Brussels, Belgium, pp. 647–650, doi.org/10.1109/IGARSS47720.2021.9553237. This tool extends that method to both ice sheets with a larger dataset and a Bayesian model; see docs/1_rssnr_background.md.

For design exploration only. Model predictions carry substantial uncertainty ( cross-validated RMSE) and the link budget makes assumptions that may not hold for any particular system. Verify against the original data before relying on a number.
